How to decide: get back together or move on

The pull to reunite is often nostalgia for the good moments rather than evidence the relationship would work this time. The decisive question is whether the specific thing that ended it has genuinely changed — not whether you still have feelings. Feelings are common; change is rare.

Separate missing the person from wanting the relationship back. Loneliness, habit, and an idealized memory can all masquerade as “we’re meant to be.” What would actually be different this time, concretely?

  • What changed — has the real cause of the breakup actually shifted, on either side?
  • Pattern vs. one-off — was it a fixable moment or a recurring dynamic?
  • Nostalgia check — do you miss this relationship, or just not being alone?
  • Respect and trust — can these be rebuilt, realistically, or were they the problem?
  • Your growth — have you both changed enough that the old ending wouldn’t repeat?

Common questions

Should I get back with my ex or move on?

Reconsider only if the specific cause of the breakup has genuinely changed and trust can be rebuilt. Move on when you mainly miss the person or an idealized memory, or the same dynamic would simply repeat.

How do I know if getting back together is a good idea?

Ask what would concretely be different this time. If you can’t name a real change on either side, the pull is probably nostalgia rather than a reason to reunite.

Is it nostalgia or real love?

Nostalgia misses the highlights of the past; a real reason to reunite points to specific change in the present. If you’re only recalling good moments, it’s likely the former.
