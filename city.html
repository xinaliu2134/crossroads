How to decide: big city or hometown

This decision is really about which life you’re optimizing for over the next few years — not which place is “better.” Big cities tend to buy career optionality, density of opportunity, and anonymity at the cost of money, space, and proximity to your existing support. Hometowns flip that trade.

Be concrete and time-boxed. The right answer at 25 with a career to build can differ from the right answer when family, cost of living, and quality of daily life weigh more. Compare the actual cities in front of you on the factors that move your life.

  • Career trajectory — how much does the bigger market actually accelerate your field?
  • Real cost of living — net of higher pay, are you ahead or behind month to month?
  • Support network — the value of family and friends nearby is easy to underrate until you need it.
  • Daily life — commute, space, and pace shape happiness more than a city’s reputation.
  • Reversibility — treat it as a 2–3 year experiment you can revisit, not a permanent verdict.

Common questions

Should I move to a big city or stay in my hometown?

Move when the bigger market meaningfully accelerates your career and you value optionality over proximity to support. Stay when cost of living, family, and daily quality of life matter more than raw opportunity right now.

Is it worth moving to a big city for a career?

Often yes early in a career, when density of opportunity compounds — but only if the higher pay actually outpaces the higher cost of living for your situation.

How do I choose where to live?

Compare the specific places on career trajectory, real (net) cost of living, support network, and daily life — and treat the move as a revisitable experiment rather than a forever decision.
