How to decide: startup or job

The honest version of this question is not “am I a founder or an employee?” but “is now the right time for me?” It’s a timing call, not an identity one — plenty of strong founders worked a job first to build savings, skills, and a network. Taking a job today does not close the startup door.

Work through the factors below honestly. If most point one way, you have your answer. If they’re split, that’s exactly where the quiz helps — it weighs them against each other instead of letting the loudest feeling win.

  • Runway — how many months can you live with little or no income? Under six is a danger zone.
  • Risk tolerance — judge it by how you behaved in past uncertainty, not how you wish you behaved.
  • Idea maturity — can you name the customer and problem in one sentence? If not, it’s a feeling, not a startup yet.
  • Obligations — dependents and debt raise the runway you need and lower your tolerance for a bad month.
  • The real options — compare the actual job offer vs. the actual idea, not the fantasy versions.

Common questions

Should I start a startup or get a job?

Start when you have 12–18 months of runway, a specific problem you understand deeply, and real tolerance for uncertainty. Take a job when you need stability now, your idea is still vague, or you want to learn the industry and build savings first.

How much should I save before starting a business?

A common rule of thumb is 12–18 months of essential expenses plus whatever the business needs to reach first revenue. With dependents or debt, lean longer — or build it as a side project first.

What is the right age to start a startup?

There is no single right age; the average high-growth founder is around 40 at launch. Runway, idea maturity, and obligations matter far more than age.
