How to decide: buy or rent

Buying isn’t automatically “throwing money away on rent,” and renting isn’t automatically smarter. The decision turns on how long you’ll stay, the true cost of owning, and what you’d do with the money you don’t tie up in a down payment.

The single biggest factor is time horizon. Buying carries large upfront and transaction costs that take years to earn back, so a short stay usually favors renting even when the monthly numbers look similar.

  • How long you’ll stay — the shorter the horizon, the more renting wins; buying needs years to break even.
  • True cost of owning — not just the mortgage: taxes, insurance, maintenance, and closing/selling costs.
  • Opportunity cost — what your down payment could earn if invested instead.
  • Flexibility — renting keeps you mobile for jobs and life changes; owning ties you down.
  • Stability and control — owning fixes your housing cost and lets you make the place yours.

Common questions

Should I buy or rent a home?

Buy when you’ll stay long enough to earn back the upfront and transaction costs (often several years) and value stability. Rent when your horizon is short, you need flexibility, or your down payment would do more invested elsewhere.

Is renting really throwing money away?

No. Renting buys flexibility and frees capital you’d otherwise lock in a down payment. Owning has its own “wasted” costs too — interest, taxes, maintenance, and transaction fees.

How long do I need to stay for buying to be worth it?

It depends on local prices and costs, but buying generally needs several years to break even against the upfront and selling costs — the shorter your stay, the more renting tends to win.
