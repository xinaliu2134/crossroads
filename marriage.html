How to decide: get married or wait

“Are we ready?” is less about being certain and more about being aligned on the things that quietly decide a marriage: money, children, where you’ll live, and how you each handle conflict. Couples rarely regret waiting to get those conversations right; they more often regret skipping them.

Distinguish doubt about this person from nerves about the commitment itself. The first is worth taking seriously; the second is normal. Waiting is a valid, healthy choice when key alignment isn’t there yet — it isn’t the same as a no.

  • Values alignment — money, kids, location, and faith/family expectations, discussed openly.
  • Conflict style — how you repair after a fight predicts more than how often you fight.
  • Reason for the timing — readiness vs. outside pressure (age, family, social timelines).
  • Doubt, located — is it about this partner, or about commitment in general?
  • Independent lives — two people choosing to merge, not one rescuing or completing the other.

Common questions

How do I know if I should get married or wait?

Marry when you’re aligned on the big quiet decisions — money, children, location, conflict — and the timing is yours, not outside pressure. Wait when key alignment is missing or your doubt is about this specific partner.

Is it normal to be nervous before getting engaged?

Nerves about the commitment itself are common. What deserves attention is persistent doubt about this particular person or about core issues you haven’t resolved together.

Should we live together before marriage?

It can surface day-to-day compatibility and how you handle money and conflict, which are exactly the areas that matter most — but the deciding factor is honest alignment, however you get there.
